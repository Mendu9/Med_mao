Nonstandard Abbreviation and Acronym SICAO symptomatic internal carotid artery occlusion Clinical Perspective What Is New? Successful recanalization of symptomatic carotid artery occlusion in a nonacute phase could reduce the recurrence of ischemic stroke. What Are the Clinical Implications? Endovascular recanalization may provide an alternative treatment for patients with drug‐refractory nonacute carotid artery occlusion. Internal carotid artery occlusion accounts for 7.27%–14% of ischemic stroke. , 1 Endovascular treatment has been demonstrated to be effective within 24 hours of symptomatic internal carotid artery occlusion (SICAO). 2 However, the treatment for nonacute SICAO remains unsatisfactory. The 2‐year risk of ipsilateral ischemic stroke is 10%–15% while a patient receives the best medical treatment. 3 , 4 Patients with hemodynamic impairment have a higher risk of recurrent stroke. 5 , 6 Cognitive decline is also common in patients with carotid artery occlusion. 7 8 Endovascular recanalization has been considered as an alternative treatment for SICAO. Several case series reported that endovascular recanalization was feasible and could improve cerebral perfusion. 9 , 8 However, these exploratory studies are limited by their small sample sizes and discrepant findings. A retrospective analysis of 138 patients with SICAO with endovascular attempts revealed a successful recanalization rate of 61.6% and a procedural complication rate of 4.3%. 10 Another report showed a success rate of 68.8% and a perioperative complication rate of 18.8% in 32 patients. 11 Kao et al found recanalization‐associated periprocedural events could neutralize its long‐term benefits to a great extent. 12 As such, the safety and long‐term outcomes of endovascular recanalization for SICAO remain controversial. In the present study, we examined the outcomes of patients with SICAO who underwent endovascular attempts in a nationwide prospective registry. We sought to investigate whether successful recanalization, as compared with failed recanalization, could offer clinical benefits that outweigh procedural risks. Methods Study Design and Participants The data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. This study analyzed consecutive patients with endovascular recanalization of nonacute SICAO in a prospective cohort from the China Interventional Stroke Registry. China Interventional Stroke Registry is a nationwide registry of consecutive patients who underwent endovascular treatment of intracranial or extracranial atherosclerotic lesion. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Jinling Hospital and each subcenter. 13 The subjects in the China Interventional Stroke Registry were included in the analysis if they fulfilled the following criteria: (1) internal carotid artery occlusion was confirmed by digital subtraction angiography; (2) patients experienced ipsilateral ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack despite receiving medical management; (3) hemodynamic impairment was manifested as decreased cerebral blood flow or prolonged mean transit time on perfusion computed tomography/magnetic resonance imaging, poor to moderate collaterals (American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology Scale 1–2) on angiography, or watershed infarction on magnetic resonance imaging , 14 , 15 ; and (4) endovascular revascularization was conducted within a window of 1–180 days after the last ischemic event or deterioration. 16 Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) the etiology of the carotid occlusion identified as nonatherosclerosis such as moyamoya disease, vasculitis, arterial dissection, or previous head/neck radiation; (2) previous endovascular treatment or surgery on the intervened carotid artery; (3) concomitant intracranial aneurysm; (4) ipsilateral common carotid artery occlusion or middle cerebral artery occlusion; (5) patients presenting with large infarction involving >one‐third middle cerebral artery territory on computed tomography/magnetic resonance imaging scan; (6) incomplete angiographic data; and (7) a lack of follow‐up information on outcomes at 3 months. Endovascular Interventions Endovascular recanalization is a complex intervention involving various strategies and techniques used by different centers and surgeons. Here, we describe a common recanalization procedure for isolated occlusions within the proximal internal carotid artery. An 8‐F guide catheter was placed transfemorally in the common carotid artery. A 0.014‐inch microwire with intermediate stiffness (PT Graphix or PT2; Boston Scientific, United States) was used in combination with a 0.017‐inch microcatheter to pass through the occlusion. A gentle force with continuous rotating movements of the wire tip should be used while advancing. After the guidewire passed through the carotid artery, the microcatheter followed the wire and advanced to the middle cerebral artery. Angiography was performed via the microcatheter to confirm placement within the true lumen. Then an exchange‐length microwire (Transend 300 Floppy; Stryker, United States) was placed and the microcatheter was removed. Lesions were inflated with 2.0 mm balloon (Maverick2; Boston Scientific, United States). A distal embolic protection device (Spider FX, Covidien, United States) was deployed if an adequate distal landing zone could be noted. After adequate balloon dilatation, a self‐expanding stent was deployed across the occlusion lesion. All patients were sent to the intensive care unit to monitor vital signs for at least 24 hours. Systolic blood pressure was maintained within 90–140 mmHg and at about 80% of baseline level for 3 days to prevent hyperperfusion. Dual antiplatelet medication was continued for at least 6 months. Data Collection We collected patients’ demographics, clinical characteristics, and imaging characteristics. All imaging data were sent to the core angiographic laboratory in Jinling Hospital and were analyzed in a blind manner by 2 neuroradiologists. Reverse ophthalmic artery was defined as reverse contrast flow in the ophthalmic artery from the external carotid artery during the ipsilateral injection. Collateral circulation via the anterior communicating artery or the posterior communicating artery was considered present if the middle cerebral artery branches were filled by either of these on the angiogram. Collateral circulation status was evaluated by digital subtracted angiography according to the American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology scale. The internal carotid artery was segmented according to the Bouthillier segmentation method. 17 Outcome Measures Patients were prospectively followed up at 1, 3, and 6 months after the intervention and then once every 6 months by telephone or outpatient visit until June 2022. If a patient had a focal neurological deficit and/or hospitalization, the medical record was asked to be sent to independent clinical‐event adjudication committee. The committee members classified stroke types based on available medical records and imaging (magnetic resonance imaging or computed tomography scans). The primary outcome was the ipsilateral ischemic stroke recurrence. The secondary clinical outcome was all strokes and death. The technical efficacy outcome was the successful recanalization rate. Successful revascularization was defined as Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score 3 anterograde flow in the carotid artery and residual stenosis <50%. Safety outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke, major stroke, and all strokes within 30 days after recanalization. Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was identified according to the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification. 17 18 Statistical Analysis Categorical variables were displayed as n (%) and compared with the chi‐square test or Fisher's exact test. Continuous variables were expressed as means (SD) or medians (interquartile range) and compared with independent sample t ‐tests or Mann–Whitney U tests. Logistic regression was used to estimate the association between safety outcomes and successful recanalization. Proportional‐hazards assumption was confirmed by the Schoenfeld residuals test. The subdistribution hazard model was adopted to explore the association of successful recanalization with ipsilateral ischemic stroke. The cumulative incidence function was used to estimate the end point rate. Gray's test was used to compare distributions of time to the end point. If the proportional‐hazards assumption was not met, restricted mean survival time analysis was used to evaluate the effect of recanalization. Given the baseline imbalance between groups, a propensity score matched cohort was established in a 1:1 ratio to account for 6 covariates using nearest neighbor matching with a caliper width of 0.2. In multivariate analysis, model 1 adjusted for the propensity score. Model 2 adjusted for age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, coronary heart disease, smoking status, symptomatic event, time from last ischemic event to revascularization, collateral circulation status, occlusion site, and baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score. Furthermore, subgroup analysis was conducted in patients of various ages, sex, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, collateral circulation status, and occlusion site. Categories (C1; C1–C2; C4‐C5; C1–C4/C5) were reclassified as C1–C5. C4–C6/C7 occlusion was merged into C1–C6/7 occlusion. The subdistribution hazard model was used to assess the effect of recanalization on ipsilateral ischemic stroke in subgroups. A 2‐sided P value <0.05 was considered significant for all tests. All analyses were performed with Statistical Package for Social Sciences (version 26.0; IBM, Chicago, IL) and R statistical software (version 3.4.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Results Baseline Characteristics We initially screened 595 consecutive patients with SICAO undergoing endovascular treatment from January 2010 to December 2021. Among them, 84 patients were excluded according to the protocol (Figure S1 ). The final analysis involved 511 patients. Endovascular recanalization was successful in 300 patients (58.7%) and failed in 211 patients (41.3%). Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of these patients. The average age was 62±10 years old with mild neurological deficits (median [interquartile range] National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, 4 [1–8]). Patients with successful recanalization had less time from the last ischemic event to the treatment (median [interquartile range] days, 18 [12–33] versus 22 [14–50]; P =0.003), less medical history of dyslipidemia (62.3% versus 71.6%; P =0.030), and a higher proportion of stroke as the symptomatic event (91.7% versus 82.5%; P =0.002). Other clinical characteristics were not statistically different among the patients. Angiographic and Procedural Characteristics Patients with successful recanalization exhibited a higher prevalence of focal occlusion limited to either the C1–C2 segments (16.7% versus 7.1%; P <0.001) or the C6–C7 segments (15.3% versus 6.2%; P <0.001) when compared with patients with failed recanalization (Table 1 ). Reverse flow through ophthalmic artery was more frequently observed in the recanalized carotid artery (75.3% versus 60.7%; P <0.001). The incidence of good collateral circulation (American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology Scale 3–4) was lower in successfully recanalized patients than in nonrecanalized patients (26.9% versus 41.9%; P <0.001). Successful wire crossing was achieved in 67.7% (346/511) of all cases. 18.0% (54/300) of the recanalized patients were treated with balloon angioplasty alone. In contrast, 62.7% (188/300) of the recanalized patients required 1 stent, 15.0% (45/300) required 2 stents, and 4.3% (13/300) required 3 or more stents to restore a Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction grade 3 antegrade flow. Clinical Outcomes The total follow‐up time was 2106 person‐years for this cohort, with a median follow‐up of 37 months (interquartile range, 19–52). The incidence of technical complications was 9.2% (47/511) during the recanalization procedure. Among them, 28 patients had dissection, 12 patients had blood vessel perforation, and 8 patients had distal embolism. The rate of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 30 days was 3.7% (11/300) in the patients with successful recanalization and 0.5% (1/211) in the patients with failed recanalization ( P =0.047). Rates of ischemic stroke (odds ratio [OR], 0.90 [95% CI, 0.33–2.46]; =0.839), major stroke (OR, 1.75 [95% CI, 0.71–4.30]; P =0.222), and all strokes (OR, 1.91 [95% CI, 0.83–4.40]; P =0.128) within 30 days were similar between 2 groups (Table 2 ). For the primary outcome, a total of 50 recurrent ipsilateral ischemic stroke was noted in 46 patients, 15 in the success group and 35 in the failure group (Figure 1A ). Successful recanalization was associated with a significant reduction of ischemic stroke recurrence (adjusted hazard ratio [HR], 0.28 [95% CI, 0.13–0.59]; P <0.001). The 3‐year rate of ischemic stroke was 4.5% in the success group and 15.3% in the failure group (HR, 0.32 [95% CI 0.16–0.63]; P <0.001, Table 2 ). A similar reduction in the accumulative events of all strokes and death was also observed (Figure 1B ). The 3‐year rate of all strokes and death was 12.1% in the success group and 18.1% in the failure group (Δ restricted mean survival time, 1.13 months [95% CI, −0.74–3.01]; P =0.235). Subgroup Analyses The outcome of recanalization remained consistent in subgroups defined by age, sex, and baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score (Figure 2 ). For patients with poor to moderate collaterals, and patients with occlusion limited to C1–C5 or C6–C7 segments, successful recanalization was associated with a lower risk of ipsilateral ischemic stroke. Propensity Score Matching Analysis One‐to‐one propensity score matching created 151 matched pairs. The patients’ characteristics were well balanced between the 2 groups (Figure S2 and Table S1 ). Successful recanalization remained associated with a lower risk of ischemic stroke after adjusting the propensity score (adjusted HR, 0.28 [95% CI, 0.13–0.58]; P <0.001, Table S2 ). In propensity‐matched cohorts, patients with successful recanalization had a decreased 3‐year (Δ restricted mean survival time, 2.36 months [95% CI, 0.06–4.66]; P =0.044) risk of all strokes and death (Table S3 ). Discussion The current study employed a prospective multicenter registry based on China Interventional Stroke Registry to investigated consecutive patients with SICAO undergoing endovascular treatment. Successful recanalization occurred in 58.7% of patients in the 12‐year real‐world experience. Despite a higher rate of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 30 days, successful recanalization appeared to have long‐term benefits in reducing ipsilateral ischemic stroke recurrence. Patients with SICAO had a higher risk of cerebrovascular events than patients with all other degrees of stenosis. , 1 However, randomized trials on the carotid stenosis excluded patients with carotid artery occlusion. 19 , 20 , 21 Carotid stenting and carotid endarterectomy have historically shown a high success rate of nearly 100% in patients, with major complications occurring in 4.4%–7.2% of cases. 22 Therefore, it is reasonable to consider that endovascular recanalization for SICAO might offer clinical benefits if the intervention achieves comparable success in technique and perioperative outcomes. Our study reported a postprocedural 30‐day stroke rate of 7.0% in the success group. A previous meta‐analysis displayed the complication of recanalization was 5% of permanent events. 23 This level of procedural risk makes endovascular recanalization readily translatable into clinical benefits. As a counterexample, the SICAO patients who underwent extracranial–intracranial bypass surgery exhibited a postoperative morbidity and mortality rate of 15%. 24 This was sufficient to nullify any overall benefit of surgery. 25 Another noteworthy statistic is the technique success rate of 58.7%. A previous meta‐analysis involving 13 studies reported a successful recanalization rate of 72.6%. However, 10 of these studies contained <45 subjects. A publication bias should be considered when interpreting the findings. It is difficult for an intervention with a technique success rate of approximately 60% to translate into common clinical application. Take mechanical thrombectomy for example. The initial negative trials predominantly used the first‐generation thrombectomy technology including Merci and Penumbra devices, which led to a low rate of successful recanalization (27%–40%). , 26 Subsequent randomized controlled trails demonstrated the overwhelming benefits of mechanical thrombectomy using stent retrievers with a higher reperfusion rate at 72.4%–88%. 27 Thus, more efforts should be made to improve the recanalization efficacy for SICAO. There are angiographic classifications and scoring systems to predict the successful recanalization. 28 , 11 Some endovascular strategies may improve efficiency of the treatment, such as dual‐roadmap guidance, dual guiding wires technology, and staged endovascular recanalization. 29 , 30 31 Wang et al found a favorable outcome (90‐day modified Rankin scale score ≤2) occurred more often in patients with endovascular treatment compared with those undergoing medical treatment. Few studies reported the long‐term prognosis after recanalization in patients with nonacute SICAO. We previously reported the 1‐year risk of ischemic stroke of 8% (1/12). 32 Guimaraens et al reported 34 successfully recanalized patients experienced no ischemic events during a mean follow‐up period of 2.2 years. 33 These results were limited by the lack of a control group. One study reported successful recanalization, including 21 patients, could not decrease the 2‐year risk of cerebrovascular events compared with medical treatment (28.6% versus 46.3%). 34 Another research, including 118 patients, showed a significantly decreased risk of ischemic events in successful recanalization compared with the failed procedure (8% versus 24%). 35 In the present study, successful revascularization showed benefits in reducing ipsilateral ischemic stroke recurrence, as well as all strokes and death. This effect is more significant with an almost 70% reduction of risk after excluding procedural events. 36 Our study has limitations. First, the comparison between successful and failed recanalization is different from the comparison between endovascular treatment and medical treatment. Second, the current analysis was based on a prospective registry over a long time, during which the endovascular techniques, devices, and perioperative management have been dramatically improved. Our results may not reflect the current status of clinical practice. We look forward to future studies displaying improved safety and efficacy profiles of endovascular recanalization. Third, brain perfusion status was not analyzed. The patients with SICAO with inadequate cerebrovascular reserve had a higher risk of ischemic stroke. , 6 We examined the collateral circulation using American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology Scale in this study. Subgroup analyses revealed that recanalization treatment remained beneficial in patients with poor to moderate collaterals. Baseline perfusion imaging parameters may serve as a patient selection criterion in future studies. Fourth, 75% of patients received endovascular recanalization within 35 days of last ischemic event. A majority of them experienced mild neurological deficits. The applicability of our findings to cases of chronic carotid occlusion lasting over 3 months and moderate to severe strokes may be limited. 7 In conclusion, the present study provides real‐world evidence supporting the safety and long‐term efficacy of successful recanalization for patients with nonacute SICAO. We look forward to the results of clinical trials (ChiCTR2100042756 and NCT04219774 ) to identify the optimal treatment regimen for these patients.
