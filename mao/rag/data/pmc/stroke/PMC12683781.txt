Introduction Acute Ischemic Stroke (AIS) represents a formidable health challenge, imposes a substantial public-health burden owing to its high incidence, often resulting in profound disability, and a notable mortality toll. Intravenous thrombolysis treatment has been widely recognized as an important therapeutic approach for AIS [ 1 ]. However, the effectiveness of thrombolysis treatment is influenced by various factors such as time window for thrombolysis, infarct size, and collateral circulation. Therefore, accurate prediction of short-term outcomes and the development of individualized treatment strategies remain pressing challenges in medical researches [ 2 ]. Multimodal CT fusion with CT plain scan, CTP and CTA can comprehensively evaluate brain tissue, blood flow and vessel status, providing key information for thrombolytic treatment of acute ischemic stroke [ 3 – 5 ]. The purpose of this study was to establish a model to predict the short term outcome of AIS patients undergoing venous thrombolysis based on multimodal CT imaging technology.This study innovatively correlated the brain tissue structure, hemodynamics and vascular information of multimodal CT with the short-term prognosis of intravenous thrombolytic therapy, breaking through the limitation of traditional prognostic assessment relying on a single indicator or clinical experience, and built an accurate prediction model based on multimodal image data. Increasing model stability through verification can provide quantitative basis for clinical formulation of individualized thrombolysis plans, optimize treatment time window selection and reduce the risk of complications. It has both methodological innovation and clinical transformation value, and is expected to improve the accuracy of prognosis assessment and diagnosis and treatment efficiency of patients with acute ischemic stroke. Methods Study design and population A retrospective study comprising 180 patients with AIS admitted to The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen Medical College between Jan 2021 and Dec 2023. The patients were randomly allocated to into a modeling group ( n = 135) and a validation group ( n = 45) in a ratio of 3:1. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients who met the clinical diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke. (2) Patients who completed all necessary examinations and had complete clinical data upon admission. (3) Patients with stable vital signs. Exclusion criteria: (1) With severe heart, liver, or kidney diseases. (2) With malignant tumors. (3) Patients with contraindications for intravenous thrombolysis treatment. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen Medical College. The procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards set forth by the Committee on Human Experimentation and the Helsinki Declaration of 1964, as revised in 2013. Informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen Medical College for this retrospective study due to the exclusive use of de-identified patient data, which posed no potential harm or impact on patient care. This waiver was approved by the institutional review board and ethics committee of our institution in accordance with regulatory and ethical guidelines pertaining to retrospective studies. Research methods and observation indicators Based on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) [ 6 ] score, the patients in the modeling group were divided into two groups: the favorable prognosis group ( n = 78) and the unfavorable prognosis group ( n = 57). The mRS score ranges from 0 to 6, with the following interpretations: Symptom-free status (0 point); Minimal impact, with symptoms not hindering daily tasks or activities (1 point); Mild functional limitation, still capable of managing personal affairs autonomously (2 points); Moderate restriction, needing occasional assistance but maintaining independent mobility (3 points); Marked disability, requiring aid for mobility and basic self-care (4 points); Profound incapacitation, confined to bed with loss of control over bodily functions, necessitating constant care (5 points); Fatal outcome (6 points). A score of ≤ 3 is divided into good prognosis, and a score of >3 or above is divided into poor prognosis. Upon admission, all patients exhibiting symptoms suggestive of an acute anterior circulation ischaemic stroke were immediately placed on the green channel for acute ischaemic stroke treatment. Multimodal CT examinations were conducted, including: (1) Non-contrast enhanced CT (NCCT): The entire brain was scanned with the following parameters: a tube current of 300 mA, a slice thickness of 5 mm and a tube voltage of 120 kV. The objective was to exclude brain haemorrhage and tumour lesions and to ascertain the optimal level for CT perfusion imaging. (2) CT perfusion imaging (CTP): The contrast agent injection comprised 50 mL of nonionic contrast agent (iodixanol) and 30 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride solution, administered via the right elbow vein at a rate of 5 mL/s. The scanning parameters were as follows: voltage of 80 kV, current of 200 mA, slice thickness of 5 mm, and a delay of 7 s before commencing the scan. The arterial phase was scanned at two-second intervals, while the venous phase was scanned at five-second intervals, resulting in a total scanning time of approximately 60 s. The scanning range encompassed the skull base and vertex. The objective was to obtain dynamic volumetric data of the entire brain for subsequent analysis of the perfusion index. (3) Computed tomography angiography (CTA): The scanning range extended from the aortic arch to the vertex. The contrast agent was administered in two stages. The initial injection comprised 65 mL of iodixanol, followed by a further 40 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride solution, administered at a rate of 4.5–5.0.5.0 mL/s. The scanning parameters included a slice thickness of 1 mm (post-processed to 0.625 mm), voltage of 120 kV. The objective was to observe the morphology of the responsible blood vessels and determine the presence of stenosis or occlusion. The subsequent stages of the process, namely image post-processing and analysis, are described in the following section. The original images of NCCT, CTP, and CTA were transferred to the GE AW Server 4.7 workstation for subsequent post-processing. The CTP data were processed with the workstation software for the purpose of analysing a number of perfusion index parameters, including cerebral blood volume (CBV), cerebral blood flow (CBF), mean transit time (MTT), and time to peak (TTP). The CTA data were subjected to post-processing techniques, including curved reformation, volume rendering, and multi-planar reformation, with the objective of observing the vascular morphology and determining the status of the responsible blood vessels. Two radiologists with extensive experience, who were unaware of the clinical information, interpreted the NCCT, CTP, and CTA images independently. The diagnosis of the NCCT images was made using the Alberta Stroke Early CT Score.The core infarction area and ischemic penumbra were identified, and the CBV, CBF, MTT, and TTP were evaluated individually. General information scale: National Institute of Health stroke scale (NIHSS) [ 7 ] was a standardized tool widely used to assess the degree of neurological deficit in stroke patients. Penumbral measurement Cerebral perfusion volumes were automatically quantified with MIStar software (Apollo Medical Imaging Technology, Melbourne, Australia). Ischemic volume (mL) was defined as tissue with cerebral blood flow (CBF) < 30% of normal, and infarct core as tissue with time-to-maximum > 3.0 s. The penumbral fraction was calculated as (ischemic volume – infarct core)/ischemic volume, yielding a 0–1 scale. All CTP data were visually inspected to confirm that the delineated penumbra and core lay within the relevant neurovascular territory corresponding to clinical symptoms and were free of artefacts that could influence volumetry. Statistical analysis The gathered experimental data underwent analysis employing SPSS 27.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Continuous data adhering to normal distribution were represented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with comparisons facilitated by the independent samples t-test. Categorical information was presented through frequencies or percentages, with comparisons utilizing either the chi-squared (χ 2 ) test or Fisher’s exact test, depending on the data characteristics. In order to ascertain the factors that influence the short-term prognosis of AIS patients who have undergone intravenous thrombolysis, a multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted.Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, with any difference below this threshold deemed statistically noteworthy.SPSS was used to build prediction models, and R language was used to determine the application value of receiver curve (ROC), calibration curve and DCA curve. The ROC curve, 7 < AUC ≤ 0.9, indicates good predictive value of the model in the validation set Discussion AIS, as one of the important treatment methods for AIS, the prediction and evaluation of the efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis have crucial significance for clinical decision-making [ 8 ]– [ 9 ]. In recent years, with the continuous development of multimodal CT technology, it has played an increasingly important role in the diagnosis and efficacy evaluation of AIS [ 10 ]. The aim of this study is to establish a short-term prognosis model for AIS patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis based on multimodal CT and validate its effectiveness, in order to provide more scientific and accurate evidence for clinical decision-making in AIS patients. In the process of model construction, analyses were employed to select NIHSS score, CBV, CBF, MTT, and TTP as independent influencing factors for short-term prognosis of acute ischemic stroke patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis. These parameters can be readily derived from routine multimodal CT and can objectively reflect the cerebral hemodynamic status and degree of neurologic functional deficit in AIS patients, thus possessing high clinical value [ 11 ]– [ 12 ]. Multivariable logistic regression identified NIHSS score, CBV, CBF, MTT, and TTP as independent predictors of short-term outcome after intravenous thrombolysis for acute ischemic stroke. The prognostic equation is Logit(P) = (3.484×NIHSS score)+(2.975×CBV)+(2.857×CBF)+(3.129×MTT)+(3.388×TTP). The coefficient before each variable in the expression reflects the influence degree and direction of the corresponding variable on the prediction results. A positive coefficient means that an increase in the value of this variable will increase the value of Logit(P). In Logistic regression, Logit(P) has a logical relationship with the probability of event occurrence P. An increase in Logit(P) usually means an increase in the probability of poor short-term prognosis (or other conditions defined as “event occurrence”) in patients with acute ischemic stroke treated with intravenous thrombolysis. For example, the NIHSS score coefficient is 3.484, which means that for every unit increase in the NIHSS score, Logit(P) will increase by 3.484 units, and the possibility of a patient’s short-term prognosis increases. For patients with acute ischemic stroke who are scheduled to receive intravenous thrombolysis, the NIHSS score and the multimodal CT parameters CBV, CBF, MTT, and TTP are prospectively collected before treatment. These values are then entered into the model to compute Logit(P), which is subsequently transformed into the probability of a poor short-term prognosis by the Logistic function P = 1/(1 + e^-Logit(P)). Previous studies by Heitkamp C et al. [ 13 ]. have shown that CTA has certain value in predicting the success rate of recanalization in acute ischemic stroke, which is similar to the results of this study. If the calculated probability P exceeds the predefined clinical threshold, the patient is considered to be at increased risk of poor short-term outcome following intravenous thrombolysis [ 14 ]. Conversely, when P lies below the threshold, the short-term prognosis is regarded as more favorable, yet ongoing close observation remains essential to detect any clinical change [ 15 ]– [ 16 ]. During the model validation process, it was found that the calibration curve slope was close to 1 in both the M group and the V group, it shows that the predicted risks of the model are consistent with the actual risks. This further validates the effectiveness and reliability of the model [ 17 – 19 ]. Additionally, the ROC analysis results show that the model has good predictive ability in both the M group and the V group, with areas under the curve of 0.967 and 0.828, indicating its high clinical application value in predicting the short-term prognosis of AIS patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis [ 20 ]. The established multimodal CT-based short-term prognosis model for AIS patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis in this study provides clinicians with a more scientific and accurate predictive tool. Through this model, doctors can predict the short-term prognosis of patients after receiving intravenous thrombolysis based on such parameters as NIHSS score, CBV, CBF, MTT, and TTP, thereby providing strong support for the formulation of personalized treatment plans for patients [ 17 ]. Furthermore, the model helps improve the efficiency of medical resource utilization, reduce unnecessary medical expenses, and alleviate the economic burden on patients and their families. Clinically, doctors can choose whether to apply the model for prediction based on the specific circumstances and needs of the patients [ 18 ]. For patients with severe conditions and serious neurologic deficits, doctors can more actively adopt intravenous thrombolysis to improve their prognosis. For patients with milder conditions and mild neurologic deficits, doctors can weigh the pros and cons based on the model’s predictive results when deciding whether to use intravenous thrombolysis [ 19 ]– [ 20 ]. Moreover, the model can also be used to evaluate the effectiveness of different treatment strategies, providing more scientific evidence for clinical decision-making [ 21 – 23 ]. As medical technology advances relentlessly, the utilization of multimodal CT technology for diagnosing and assessing the efficacy of AIS (Acute Ischemic Stroke) is poised to gain wider acceptance and prevalence [ 24 ]. Looking ahead, ongoing refinements and enhancements to this model will bolster its predictive precision and credibility, ensuring more accurate outcomes [ 25 ]. Although the study demonstrates that NIHSS score, CBV, CBF, MTT, and TTP exert a significant influence on prognosis and that the model achieves good discriminative performance (AUC > 0.7), several limitations remain. First, the relatively large standard error attenuates the model’s reliability and stability in clinical application; this may stem from data imprecision or sample heterogeneity and should be addressed through more rigorous data curation and refined analytical approaches in future work. Second, the retrospective design introduces potential selection bias that could limit the generalizability of the findings; prospective studies with representative cohorts are therefore warranted. Finally, the modest sample size may inadequately capture the full spectrum of acute ischemic stroke heterogeneity; enlarging the sample and incorporating patients with diverse clinical characteristics will enhance the model’s external validity and predictive accuracy. Conclusion In conclusion, this study establishes a multimodal CT-based short-term prognosis model for acute ischemic stroke patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis and validates its effectiveness, providing a robust evidence for clinical decision-making in AIS. Future efforts will focus on further refining the model, as well as promoting its widespread application in clinical practices.
