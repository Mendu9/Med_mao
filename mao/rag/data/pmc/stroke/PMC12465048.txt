1 Introduction Stroke is the second leading cause of death globally, accounting for about 95 % of all deaths [1] . Ischemic stroke consisted of 60–70 % of all strokes around the world [2] . Intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) is well known as the standard treatment for disabling acute ischemic stroke (AIS) within 3–6 h of symptom onset [3] . However, hemorrhagic transformation (HT) remains a significant complication after IVT, with an incidence rate of 58 % [4] . The blood-brain barrier (BBB) isolates brain tissue from peripheral blood, ensuring a stable and safe intracerebral environment [5] . In AIS, the integrity of the BBB is compromised, which has been scientifically validated as a key mechanism for the occurrence of HT [6] . After IVT, HT is influenced by multiple factors, including the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, hyperglycemia, atrial fibrillation (AF), and the location of vascular occlusion, among others [7] , [8] , [9] , [10] , [11] . The occurrence of HT in AIS leads to increased mortality or a poorer state of functional recovery in patients [12] and is strongly associated with a poor prognosis [13] . CT perfusion (CTP) is commonly used to determine the suitability of AIS patients for IVT or endovascular thrombectomy [14] . During the therapeutic time window, IVT can effectively restores cerebral blood flow (CBF) within ischemic tissues [15] . Accurate and prompt patient assessment and selection for the most appropriate treatment are crucial. Some studies explored the relationship between CTP parameters and HT after IVT, but conclusive results were yet to be achieved [16] , [17] . This study aims to evaluate the diagnostic value of combining CTP parameters with clinical information for detecting HT after IVT in AIS patients. It also seeks to analyze predictors of HT and develop dynamic nomograms to assist clinicians in more accurately assessing bleeding risk after IVT, thereby reducing the risk of HT and improving patient outcomes. 2 Materials and methods 2.1 Patients’ enrollment This single-center study was approved by the local institutional review board. A total of 62 AIS patients were retrospectively included from November 2020 to December 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) age ≥ 18 years; (II) IVT within 6 h of symptom onset; (Ⅲ) head routine CT, CT angiography (CTA), and CTP were performed; (IV) head routine CT or Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) from 24 h to 7 days after IVT; (Ⅴ) patient received IVT; (Ⅵ) no HT foci seen on CT before IVT. (VII) No antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy was therapies were administered within ± 2 h before or after IVT within 24 h prior to IVT. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (Ⅰ) poor image quality such as motion artefacts, metal artefacts; (Ⅱ) history of iodine contrast allergy; (Ⅲ) no follow-up CT or MRI; (IV) missing clinical information; and (V) unclear onset time. 2.2 Collection of general clinical data Patient demographic and clinical data, including baseline characteristics, laboratory findings, and imaging results, were systematically collected for all participants. Hypertension was defined as a self-reported history of hypertension, current use of antihypertensive medication, or a baseline blood pressure measurement of ≥ 140/90 mm Hg [18] . Blood pressure after IVT refers to the initial systolic and diastolic readings recorded following IVT. Diabetes was defined as a self-reported history of diabetes, current use of antidiabetic medication, or a fasting blood glucose concentration ≥ 7.0 mmol/L at the first encounter [19] . AF was identified based on a self-reported history of persistent AF or confirmed by ECG findings [20] . A history of hyperlipidemia was established if a patient had a prior diagnosis, was currently receiving lipid-lowering therapy, or had a documented serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol level > 3.37 mmol/L, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol level < 1.04 mmol/L, or triglyceride level > 1.7 mmol/L at any point [21] . Individuals were classified as smokers if they had a current or past history of tobacco consumption [22] . Prior to admission anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy prior to admission was defined as the regular use of antiplatelet agents, vitamin K antagonists, direct oral anticoagulants, or heparinoids [23] . Admission blood glucose was determined using the first measurement obtained within 24 h of hospitalization [24] . The severity of neurological deficits was assessed using the NIHSS [25] at four key time points: upon admission, immediately before IVT, at 2 h after IVT, and at discharge. Additionally, the location of vascular occlusion was identified by imaging (e.g., CTA) and classified as MCA, distal ICA, or a normal MCA-M1 segment. Finally, time to reperfusion was defined as the interval from the documented onset of stroke symptoms to the restoration of CBF [26] . 2.3 Data acquisition CT scan was performed on a 256-slice CT scanner (Revolution, General Electric Healthcare, USA). The unenhanced CT acquisition parameters were as follows: 120kVp, auto mAs, slice thickness = 5 mm. CT plain scanning was performed, followed by one-stop perfusion scanning with CTP and CTA for head lesions. The CTP acquisition parameters were as follows: 80 kVp, 200 mA, slice thickness = 5 mm, matrix = 512 × 512 mm, FOV = 230 mm × 230 mm, rotation time = 0.5 s, collimation = 128 mm × 0.625 mm. Forty milliliters of non-ionic contrast agent (Ioversol, 350mgI/mL) was injected into the elbow vein using a dual-syringe high-pressure injector at a flow rate of 4 mL/s. Thirty milliliters of saline was then injected, and the scan was started 5-s delay after the injection, with 20 cycles. The total scan time for one patient was 34 s. MRI was performed on a 3.0 T scanner [Discovery 750w, General Electric Healthcare), (Trio, Siemens), (Achieva, Philips Healthcare)]. Supplementary Table 1 summarizes the collection methods and parameters of the MRI sequences. 2.4 Image interpretation CTP images were transferred to the GE AW 4.7 workstation and processed using the CTP 4D software (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, Wisconsin). The anterior cerebral artery was designated as the input artery, whereas the superior sagittal sinus was identified as the outflow vein. Cerebral blood volume (CBV), CBF, mean transit time (MTT), time to peak (TTP), time-to-maximum (Tmax), and permeability surface area product (PS) were calculated [12] . Before IVT, Tmax maps was used to select regions of interest (ROIs) with diameters ranging from 10 to 15 mm within the ischemic lesion. Mirror image of the ischemic lesion was applied to obtain contralateral normal CTP parameters. To minimize interference from inter-patient heterogeneity [27] , a series of relative values were calculated. These included relative CBV (rCBV), relative CBF (rCBF), relative MTT (rMTT), residual function TTP (rTTP), relative Tmax (rTmax), and relative PS (rPS), which were derived by dividing the measurement of the region of interest (ROI) in the pathological hemisphere by that in the asymptomatic hemisphere. In the HT group, ROIs were identified from the follow-up CT or MRI scans showing HT (see Fig. 1 ), while in the non-HT group, measurements were taken from the core infarct area. The follow-up CT/MRI images were evaluated randomly by two radiologists with more than 7 years of working experience who were blinded to the clinical information. HT was defined as new-onset HT lesions, occurring either within or outside the infarct zone, that were evident on follow-up CT or MRI during hospital stay but not present on the admission scan [28] . Fig. 1 2.5 Statistical analysis Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 22.6, IBM Corp) and R (Version 4.4.1). Intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) were used to evaluate the consistency among all CTP measurements and observers: ICCs less than 0.5 indicated poor consistency; 0.5–0.75 indicated moderate consistency; 0.75–0.9 indicated good consistency; and greater than 0.9 indicated excellent consistency. The normality of the continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normal distributed continuous variables were expressed as means with standard deviations, and categorical variables are expressed as frequency counts and percentages. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range). Comparisons of clinical baseline characteristics and CTP parameters were conducted using the Student's t -test, Mann-Whitney U test, and chi-square test. Binary logistic regression analysis was performed to obtain combined variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to assess the predictive ability of CTP parameters and clinical information for HT after IVT. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), cut-off values, sensitivity, specificity, and Youden's index were calculated. Differences in AUCs among parameters were compared using DeLong's test. After adjusting for variables in the group comparisons and identifying those with p -values < 0.05, a forward stepwise regression approach is adopted for multivariate logistic regression analysis. A nomogram model was constructed to evaluate the predictive value of HT. Internal validation was performed using the Bootstrap resampling method, and the model's predictive performance was assessed by calculating the concordance index. The clinical utility of the model was assessed through the application of calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA). Fig. 6 4 Discussion The study established a web-based dynamic nomogram for predicting HT in AIS patients underwent IVT. The predictive model with best performance included rPS, NIHSS score before IVT, and AF. In present study, rPS was acted as the most effective CTP predictor of HT. PS indicted the rate at which contrast agent leaked from the intravascular space into the extravascular space through the compromised BBB [29] . An increase in PS typically reflects elevated BBB permeability which is commonly associated with HT and patients at risk of HT [30] . To minimize the interference arising from inter-patient heterogeneity, the rPS is typically calculated as the ratio of the average PS on the affected side to that on the unaffected side [27] . rPS can effectively measure the difference of permeability ratio between the affected and unaffected blood vessels in AIS [31] . Li et al. [13] measured both rPS and PS and found out that rPS provided a more accurate prediction of HT compared to PS. The increase of rPS ratio indicated a heightened degree of BBB disruption [32] which was consistent with the current study that rPS acted as a crucial risk factor for predicting HT. Xu et al. [17] determined that rCBF was the most effective CTP parameter for predicting HT after IVT, with superior sensitivity and specificity compared to rPS and rCBV. The differences in our findings may be attributed to variations in inclusion criteria. In our study, CTP scans were conducted within 6 h after the appearance of symptoms, whereas Xu et al.'s inclusion criterion specified that CTP examinations were to be performed 24 h after the onset of illness. Furthermore, rCBF may not fully explain the dynamic changes in microcirculatory impairment and compensatory vasodilation, particularly within the time window of cerebral infarction. Furthermore, Xuemei An et al. [33] conducted a meta-analysis and found that the NIHSS score is a reliable predictor of HT after IVT, which was in accordance with our results. In our study, the NIHSS score before IVT had the highest AUC and a cut-off value of 4.5 was effective in predicting HT in AIS patients. The NIHSS score before IVT, unaffected by factors such as thrombolytic therapy, accurately reflected the current severity of stroke and neurological deficit status of the patients. In contrast, NIHSS score at admission could be disturbed due to the unstable condition shortly after onset. Since the NIHSS score after IVT and NIHSS score at discharge were assessed at different stages after treatment and rehabilitation, the neurological function of patients might have recovered to some extent at that time, thus evaluating more about the recovery state after treatment rather than the severity of the initial stroke state. Therefore, we emphasized the importance of the NIHSS score before IVT. In the current study, we found that AF increased the risk of HT occurrence after IVT, which was consistent with previous study [34] . However, other study indicated that HT in AF patients after IVT did not show association with the existence of HT, while AF patients did have a higher mortality rate and a higher risk of intracranial HT [35] . AF patients were more susceptible to the formation of large and old thrombi, which were detached and could cause blockages local cerebrovascular, and were not easily dissolved by thrombolytic agents [36] . Therefore, AF examination before IVT was important for evaluating HT severity and predicting treatment outcomes. The current study indicated optimal performance of combined model 1 than other models. The integration of clinical information with CTP parameters is crucial for conducting comprehensive and accurate risk assessments. The clinical information of NIHSS score before IVT [37] and AF [38] have been proven to be independent risk factors for HT after IVT in AIS patients and CTP parameters also acted as important predictors of HT in AIS [39] . The rPS derived from CTP can help better identify AIS patients at higher risk of symptomatic HT following IVT compared with traditional CTP parameters such as CBF, CBV, Tmax [40] . A previous study established similar combined model of clinical information and CTP parameters to predict HT after IVT in patients with AIS also demonstrated the best predictive value of combined model [41] . Another prior study focused on HT after IVT in AIS patient also indicated highly effective predictive value of the combined model including clinical data, spectral CT and CTP parameters [42] . The combination of integrated spectral CT and CTP parameters enabled better leverage parameter measurements such as iodine concentration, which reflect the cerebral blood perfusion status [42] . Although we involved only CTP to reflect permeability, which had a broader clinical applicability compared to spectral CT, the consistent results yielded from our study with previous studies [41] , [42] also suggested the reliability of the combined model. Regarding certain predictive factors that are frequently discussed in the literature, such as N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-pro-BNP), these were not selected for inclusion in our model. Although the prediction model developed by Chen et al. [43] incorporates blood biomarkers including NT-pro-BNP, our study design was focused on integrating imaging parameters with clinically accessible variables. Current evidence indicates that NT-pro-BNP levels are associated with the severity of cardiovascular disease [44] ; however, this marker is not yet routinely assessed in the emergency management of stroke, which limits its data availability. Furthermore, our model already includes atrial fibrillation as an indicator of cardioembolic risk. Additionally, Ming Liu et al. [45] reported that prior anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy and admission blood glucose were not statistically significant ( p > 0.05), which is consistent with our findings. We speculate that the non-significance of prior medication use may stem from the low prevalence of such treatments in our cohort, leading to insufficient statistical power. As for admission blood glucose, its lack of significance might be attributed to the heterogeneity of the stress response upon admission and potential dietary confounders. Although blood pressure variability after thrombectomy has been demonstrated to be associated with reperfusion injury [46] , our study specifically focused on the pathophysiological mechanisms of HT after IVT. Moreover, after the procedural blood pressure exhibits dynamic fluctuations over time [47] , and this variable was therefore not included in our current analytical framework. Future prospective studies are warranted to investigate the role of blood pressure variability in the context of both after IVT and after thrombectomy settings. In our study, a traditional nomogram model was developed for visually predicting the probability of HT occurrence. The nomogram was a graphical tool that integrated and displayed multiple predictive indicators on a single plane, utilizing scaled lines to clearly illustrate relationships among various variables [48] . It presented an easy and straightforward method for individual predictions. This prediction method effectively transforms traditional regression models into a visual assessment of in-dividual risks, thereby enhancing practicality and accuracy [49] . Furthermore, a web-based dynamic nomogram model was utilized to further assess the risk of HT following IVT. As the complexity of parameters incorporated into the model increased, the process of utilizing traditional nomograms in static graphical versions could become cumbersome [50] . Alternatively, with the web-based dynamic nomogram, physicians were able to access to the website ( https://ctpclinicalnomogram.shinyapps.io/DynNomapp/ ) using mobile devices or computers to directly obtain prediction of possible HT after IVT in AIS patients. The application of dynamic nomogram model can help quick and accurate clinical decision-making. Our study had several limitations. Firstly, the sample size was relatively small. Larger sample size from multicenter hospital is need for future studies. Secondly, the types of HT were not analyzed in this study. Thirdly, this paper primarily focuses on discussing CTP parameters. Other factors that might influence IVT after HT, including the type and dosage of IVT agents and admission NT-pro BNP levels, were not comprehensively considered in the current study. Incorporating these variables may provide a more comprehensive assessment of HT prediction. Fourthly, CT or MRI was used to assess HT after IVT in our study due to patients’ IVT after situation, but it might not have been possible to achieve consistent evaluations of HT. Fifth, the inclusion of patients who underwent mechanical thrombectomy following IVT without systematic exclusion introduces a potential confounding factor in the analysis. This limitation will be rigorously addressed through a stratified cohort design in future prospective studies. Sixth, the follow-up imaging examination window in this study was extended up to 7 days, which may introduce minor time-related bias. This is an area that can be optimized in future research, for instance, by implementing stricter control over the follow-up time points. Finally, future studies could benefit from advanced machine learning techniques to analyze and explore CTP parameters. 5 Conclusions In conclusion, the combination of rPS with NIHSS score before IVT and AF performed better in the prediction of HT. The dynamic nomogram including these parameters demonstrated strong differentiation and consistency, offering an intuitive method to predict HT risk in AIS patients with IVT.
