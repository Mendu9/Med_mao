Introduction Endovascular thrombectomy has been recognized as the established treatment protocol for acute large vessel occlusion (LVO) in ischemic stroke [ . Current clinical guidelines have strict criteria for its application, recommending it for patients without large core infarction (LCI) 1 - 5 ] [ . The most widely accepted criterion for assessing the extent of core infarction following anterior circulation large vessel occlusion is the Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS; values range from 0 to 10, with lower values indicating larger ischemic infarction) 6 ] [ . Typically, an ASPECTS score of less than 6 is considered indicative of a large core infarction. Recent prospective clinical randomized controlled trials (RESCUE-Japan LIMIT, ANGEL-ASPECT, SELECT-2, TENSION, and LASTE) have demonstrated that ischemic stroke patients with LCI who undergo EVT have a better prognosis at 90 days compared to patients receiving SMT alone. However, among the five randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on thrombectomy for large core infarctions, four included only patients with internal carotid artery (ICA) occlusions or M1 segment occlusions. Only the LASTE and SELECT-2 studies included a very small number of patients with M2 segment occlusions 7 ] [ . Consequently, it remains uncertain whether EVT is safe for patients presenting with large ischemic cores resulting from occlusions in more distal vessels 8 - 12 ] [ . Simultaneously, previous studies have shown that the prognosis of patients with M2 occlusion underwent EVT is not inferior to patients with ICA and M1 proximal occlusion, a previous meta-analysis also reported that occlusion of the ICA is a predictor of poor prognosis 13 ] [ . The evidence from RCTs currently lacks clarity regarding the actual benefits of EVT in clinical practice for patients with large core infarctions with occlusions at different sites. Therefore, in this subgroup analysis of the MAGIC registry, we tested the hypothesis that the effectiveness and safety of EVT compared with SMT alone on clinical outcomes differs depending on the occlusion sites. 15 – 18 ] Methods Study design and patients selection This study is a subanalysis of a prospective, multicenter cohort study of early treatment in an acute stroke registry, focusing on patients who received treatment between 1 November 2021 and 8 February 2023. The MAGIC registry is an ongoing nationwide initiative that serves as a prospective observational registry for individuals with acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusion. Patients were required to present within 24 h of their last known well in China. Local committees at participating hospitals also granted approval for this study’s conduct. All enrolled patients or their legally authorized representatives provided written informed consent before enrollment. To be included in this analysis, participants had to meet specific criteria: (1) age ≥18 years; (2) acute ischemic stroke caused by anterior circulation large vessel occlusion—defined as occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA), M1 or M2 segment of the middle cerebral artery, as determined by a central laboratory on Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA); (3) substantial ischemic core evident on noncontrast computed tomography showing Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score ≤5; (4) symptom onset occurred within 24 h prior presentation unless precise time impossible. Patients meeting any one of these exclusion criterion would not become subjects: (1) prestroke modified Rankin Scale score >2; (2) lack follow-up data regarding outcomes after 90 days; (3) serious or terminal illness that was not related to acute ischemic stroke. Outcomes The primary outcome measure was the score on the modified Rankin scale (mRS) at 90 days, with the primary objective being to assess whether there was a shift in the distribution of mRS scores among the three subgroups. Secondary outcomes included comparisons of dichotomized mRS scores at 90 days, namely: 0 or 1 versus 2–6, 0–2 versus 3–6, 0–3 versus 4–6. Safety outcomes encompassed symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage occurring within 48 h of randomization, as defined by the Heidelberg hemorrhage classification (the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score increase ≥4 points) [ , death within 90 days from the onset of stroke, cerebral hernia during hospitalization, and the need for decompressive craniectomy. 35 ] HIGHLIGHTS Our subgroup analysis of prospective multicenter registry study data indicated that the clinical outcomes of stroke patients with large core infarcts undergoing endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) were associated with the site of occlusion. Specifically, patients with occlusions in the cervical internal carotid artery and M1 segment benefit more significantly from EVT compared to those with M2 segment occlusions. However, all patients showed a markedly increased probability of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage following EVT. Although several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) for large core infarcts have demonstrated efficacy in selected patients, these studies have not conducted in-depth research on patients with large core infarctions in different occlusion sites. Especially in real-world interventional practices, the effectiveness and safety of EVT for patients with large core infarcts at different occlusion sites (especially beyond the standards of RCT studies) remain uncertain. Statistical analysis Descriptive statistics were conducted to compare the populations treated with EVT and SMT at each of the three occlusion sites. The normality of continuous variables was tested using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Non-normal continuous variables and ordinal variables were reported as medians or interquartile ranges (IQRs), while categorical variables were presented as absolute numbers and percentages. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare non-normal continuous variables, while the χ 2 test or Fisher exact test was used for categorical variables. The primary outcome variable was the adjusted common odds ratio, representing the shift in the direction of improved mRS scores. Multivariable ordinal logistic regression was used to calculate the common odds ratio, adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age, baseline NIHSS (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale), baseline ASPECTS, onset-to-imaging diagnosis time, ASITN/SIR grade (ASITN/SIR grade represents cerebral collateral compensatory ability, 0–1 represents no or very poor collateral compensatory ability, 2 represents partial or moderate collateral compensatory ability, and 3–4 represents good or excellent compensatory ability), intravenous thrombolysis, serum glucose at presentation, and systolic blood pressure at presentation. A parallel line test was used to ensure the reliability of ordinal regression. Additionally, multivariable ordinal regression analysis with an interaction term was performed to examine the modification of treatment effect by occlusion sites (ICA versus M1 versus M2). For the secondary outcome, previous relevant studies defined good to moderate functional dependence in patients with large ischemic cores as an mRS score of 0 to 3 [ . Therefore, secondary favorable outcomes in this analysis included 90-day mRS scores of 0–1, 0–2, and 0–3. Generalized binary logistic models were utilized to analyze favorable dichotomized mRS outcomes, and the adjusted odds ratios with their corresponding 95% CIs were reported. 19 ] Regarding safety outcomes and serious adverse events, EVT was compared with SMT alone according to the occlusion site using the χ 2 test or Fisher’s exact test. To address potential confounding and biases inherent in observational studies, we employed inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) using the propensity score and propensity score matching (PSM) as robustness checks. In the IPTW cohort, a logistic regression model was used to estimate the propensity score, which included variables displaying baseline statistical differences for each occlusion site and those related to the outcomes. Each patient was assigned a weight inversely proportional to the probability of receiving the treatment they actually received. This method aims to create a pseudo-population in which the distribution of measured baseline covariates is independent of treatment assignment. The weighted sample was then used to estimate the treatment effect on primary and secondary outcomes using weighted regression models. Meanwhile, PSM methods were used to balance prognostic important factors. We performed a 1:1 matching based on the nearest neighbor matching with a 0.2 caliper. After conducting the PSM, we also analyzed the primary outcomes and secondary outcomes. Furthermore, multivariable binary logistic regression was employed to examine the associated factors with favorable outcomes and mortality at 90 days in the EVT group. We explored the marginal effects of each occluded site EVT group and the SMT group on age, admission serum blood glucose, admission systolic blood pressure, and baseline NIHSS. We used a logistic regression model considering interaction terms to assess the impact of surgical and nonsurgical groups on different occlusion sites. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0 (IBM) and R version 4.3.0. All P were two-sided, with statistical significance at <0.05. Additionally, 392 (52.6%) had an M1 occlusion, with 233 (59.4%) in the EVT group and 159 (40.6%) in the SMT group. Secondary outcomes Our analysis did not reveal any significant interaction between treatment modality and occlusion site in terms of favorable dichotomized mRS outcomes, with or without confounding factor adjustment (Table 2 ). In patients with M1 and ICA occlusions, those who underwent EVT demonstrated significantly better outcomes at 90 days compared to those treated with SMT alone. Among patients with M2 occlusions, we found that patients may benefit from EVT when a good outcome was defined as a 90-day mRS score of 0 to 2 (unadjusted OR, 4.78, 95% CI: 1.00–22.95; P =0.05). However, this effect was not significant after adjustment for confounders (adjusted OR, 6.06; 95% CI: 0.69–53.06; P =0.10). Comparable secondary efficacy outcomes were observed following PSM and in the IPTW cohort (Supplemental Digital Content Table S7, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). Safety and serious adverse events The safety profile of EVT compared to SMT was evaluated across three occlusion groups (Table 3 ). In the ICA occlusion and M2 occlusion cohorts, there was no significant difference in 90-day mortality between the EVT and SMT groups. However, in the M1 occlusion group, EVT demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in 90-day mortality compared to SMT. Subgroup analysis To assess the influence of EVT on patients with LCI across varying occlusion sites, we conducted a subgroup analysis that incorporated partially mismatched baseline data and implemented confounding factor controls. The results of the subgroup analysis are shown in (Supplemental Digital Content Figures S1–S3, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ) and basically support the primary analysis. The interaction between treatment modality and ASPECTS was identified in the ICA occlusion group (Supplemental Digital Content Fig. S1, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). Specifically, EVT was associated with improved prognosis in patients with ASPECTS ranging from 3 to 5 (adjusted OR, 5.00; 95% CI: 1.74–14.36). Meanwhile, EVT was also associated with improved prognosis in patients with lower admission systolic blood pressure and younger age. In the M1 occlusion group, EVT was significantly associated with favorable outcomes (Supplemental Digital Content Fig. S2, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). However, it was observed that patients in the M2 occlusion group who underwent EVT did not demonstrate improved outcomes (Supplemental Digital Content Fig. S3, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). Exploratory analysis We further conducted exploratory analyses of EVT groups at different occlusive sites to identify independent predictors of 90-day good outcome (defined as 90-day mRS 0-3) and safe outcome (defined as the occurrence of sICH or death at 90 days). In patients with ICA occlusion, we found that age, baseline NIHSS score, and serum glucose value at admission were independent predictors of poor 90-day prognosis ( P <0.05). The ASPECTS (3–5), collateral score (ASITN-SIR grade), and recanalization level (mTICI) were independent predictors of a good 90-day prognosis ( P <0.05). The predictors of the M1 occlusion group were almost the same as those of the ICA occlusion group. Additionally, we found that admission serum glucose was an independent predictor of sICH in patients with ICA and M1 occlusions. Serum blood glucose value and onset to puncture diagnosis time ( > 360 min) were independent predictors of poor 90-day prognosis in M2 occlusion group treated with EVT ( P <0.05) (Supplemental Digital Content Tables S2, S3, S4, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). Based on the analysis of marginal effects, we found that as age, serum glucose, admission systolic blood pressure, and admission NIHSS increase, the effectiveness of EVT or SMT decreases gradually for three occlusion sites, while the ICA and M1 occlusion groups overall benefit significantly from EVT, whereas the M2 occlusion group overall shows no significant benefit (Supplemental Digital Content Figs S5–S8, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). Discussion We conducted this subgroup analysis based on the MAGIC registry, aiming to determine the difference in prognosis between EVT and SMT alone in patients with LCI at different occlusive sites. Firstly, we found that there were differences in the effectiveness of EVT among patients with LCI depending on the location of occlusion. Specifically, within the subgroup of patients with M1 occlusion and ICA occlusion, patients treated by EVT experienced significantly improved outcomes at 90 days compared to those who received SMT alone. Secondly, in patients with occlusion of the M2 segment, EVT did not show a significant association with good or excellent functional outcomes when compared to SMT alone. Lastly, we found a higher incidence of sICH in patients with LCI who received EVT. The primary outcome analysis revealed a significant benefit in terms of overall functional independence within 90 days after EVT treatment for patients with ICA or the M1 segment occlusion. Consistently, the secondary outcomes, including excellent functional outcome (mRS 0–1) at 90 days, functionally independent outcome (mRS 0–2), and independent walking ability (mRS 0–3), were higher in the EVT group, which supports the results of several recent RCT studies on EVT in patients with LCI [ . In line with the findings of the TENSION study, our results demonstrate a significantly lower mortality rate in patients with M1 occlusion who received EVT compared to those treated with SMT alone 8 - 12 ] [ . 10 ] Secondly, our study revealed that patients with LCI and M2 occlusion did not experience significant benefits from EVT. The STRATIS registry study previously indicated that EVT for M2 occlusions resulted in similar recanalization success rates and positive outcomes compared to EVT for M1 occlusions [ . A post-hoc analysis of the HERMES study demonstrated the efficacy of EVT in patients with proximal M2 occlusions 20 ] [ . Several meta-analyses have suggested that while EVT for M2 occlusions may elevate the risk of surgical complications, the overall benefits outweigh the risks 16 ] [ . However, a meta-analysis focusing on EVT in mild ischemic stroke did not establish a significant link between EVT for M2 occlusions and favorable prognoses 21 ],[ 22 ] [ . These conflicting findings underscore the ongoing debate surrounding the use of EVT for treating M2 segment occlusions. 23 ] Variability in the effectiveness of treating M2 occlusions may be influenced by various factors. Firstly, patients with M2 occlusions often present with more localized areas of ischemia, which could potentially decrease the efficacy of EVT when compared to M1 or ICA occlusions [ . Secondly, the recanalization process for M2 segment occlusions may pose greater challenges due to smaller vessel diameters and increased anatomical variability, potentially contributing to lower rates of successful reperfusion. It was observed in our study that the postoperative reperfusion success rate among patients with M2 occlusions was significantly lower than that of patients with M1 occlusions (M1: 90.6%, M2: 80.4%, 14 ] P <0.05). Previous research has indicated that achieving complete recanalization is closely linked to favorable outcomes in patients with M2 occlusions, although our exploratory analysis (Supplemental Digital Content Table S3, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ) did not yield the same conclusion [ . Third, some patients with secondary M2 occlusion may have influenced the effectiveness of EVT, as confirmed by the previous discussion of the prognosis of primary and secondary M2 occlusion undergoing EVT 24 – 28 ] [ . Our subgroup analysis of the M2 occlusion group suggested that there were patients with ASPECTS ≤2, as confirmed by imaging and medical history (Fig. S3, Supplemental Digital Content 2, 34 ] http://links.lww.com/JS9/D501 ). It was considered that the thrombus in the terminal of the ICA or M1 segment spontaneously dissolved and escaped distally, resulting in secondary M2 occlusion [ . In summary, the efficacy of EVT appears to differ among various types of M2 segment occlusions. Results from several ongoing RCT studies on endovascular thrombectomy for medium and distal arteries are forthcoming ( 33 ] NCT05029414 ; NCT05152524 ; NCT05151172 ; NCT05030142 ). We hope these findings will enhance our understanding of the impact of EVT in this specific patient subgroup. It is intriguing to note that the reperfusion success rate in the ICA occlusion group is also lower compared to the M1 occlusion group (M1: 90.6%, ICA: 83.0%, P <0.05), yet it still demonstrates significant benefits from EVT. Several factors may account for this discrepancy. Firstly, while the reperfusion success rate in the ICA occlusion group is lower, it aligns with the recanalization rate documented in previous randomized controlled trials, which may be adequate to achieve sufficient reperfusion for enhancing patient prognosis. Secondly, ICA occlusion typically results in a broader region of cerebral ischemia, allowing EVT to exert a significant impact on functional prognosis even with a relatively lower reperfusion success rate. Thirdly, the hemodynamic implications of ICA occlusion may be more pronounced than those of M2 occlusion, potentially yielding positive outcomes for the patient despite incomplete reperfusion. Earlier investigations into predictive factors for EVT in patients with extensive core infarction have underscored a strong correlation between complete recanalization and improved prognosis [ . Our exploratory analysis corroborates this link, suggesting that for individuals with ICA and M1 occlusions, striving for higher recanalization grades during EVT may enhance patient outcomes. Nonetheless, it is vital to recognize that while recanalization grade appears to impact prognosis, not all patients achieving complete recanalization necessarily experience a favorable outcome 29 , 30 ] [ . Pursuing a higher grade of recanalization may be accompanied by an increased number of thrombectomy passes. Previous studies have confirmed that excessive thrombectomy attempts are associated with a higher incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and relatively poorer outcomes 31 ],[ 38 ] [ . For patients with large core infarctions, this phenomenon may be even more pronounced. This underscores the importance of considering various factors beyond vascular recanalization, such as patient age, collateral score, serum glucose values, baseline NIHSS score, among others, when assessing the efficacy of EVT comprehensively. For patients with M2 segment occlusions, we identified only serum glucose levels and prolonged diagnosis-to-puncture time ( > 360 min) as independent risk factors for poor outcomes. This may be attributed to the relatively small sample size of the included patients. Future research is needed to thoroughly investigate the prognostic factors in this patient subgroup, which will aid clinical surgeons in better managing the entire surgical process and ultimately improve patient outcomes. 27 ] The reported incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in previous RCTs ranged from 0.6 to 9.6% [ . This significant variation raises concerns about the generalizability of these findings to the real world, especially since these studies have not extensively described the incidence probabilities across different occlusion sites. Hence, our study also revealed a significant increase in the incidence of sICH following EVT in patients with LCI, with varying rates observed across different occlusion sites. Specifically, the ICA and M2 occlusion groups who were treated with EVT exhibited a higher incidence of sICH, whereas the M1 occlusion group had a relatively lower incidence of hemorrhage. The differential rates of sICH may be attributed to the distinct reactivity of occluded sites to EVT and the hemodynamic shifts postvascular recanalization. Patients with M2 occlusions may be more susceptible to reperfusion injury and hemorrhagic transformation following EVT due to the intricacies of vascular anatomy 8 – 12 ] [ . At the same time, the increased difficulty of EVT also leads to an increase in the number of passing times, which leads to an increase in bleeding events 18 ] [ . Therefore, how to reduce the incidence of sICH in patients with LCI after EVT needs more exploration in the future. 32 ] Limitation While our study provides valuable insights, it is essential to acknowledge the limitations we encountered. Firstly, although our analysis were adjusted for confounding factors, some degree of selection bias was inevitable as a real-world study. Secondly, this is a multicenter registration study within China, so only Asians are included in the study. Due to a higher prevalence of intracranial atherosclerotic disease in the Chinese population, the selection of thrombectomy techniques and the administration of antiplatelet medications postprocedure differ from those in other regions. Therefore, the applicability of the research findings to other populations may be limited. Lastly, the absence of long-term follow-up data post EVT treatment prevents a comprehensive evaluation of the treatment’s impact on patients’ long-term prognosis. Conclusions In conclusion, through subgroup analysis, our study revealed the differences in effectiveness and safety of EVT and SMT alone in the treatment of patients with acute ischemic stroke complicated with LCI. In the future, we look forward to additional studies to further validate our findings and provide more precise and personalized strategies for the treatment of patients with acute ischemic stroke combined with LCI.
