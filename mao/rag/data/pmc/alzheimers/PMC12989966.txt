Introduction Amyloid β (Aβ) is an aggregation-prone peptide and is currently the principal therapeutic target in Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Approaches targeting Aβ have mainly focused on either blocking its production or removing it from the brain. Inhibition of Aβ synthesis has been associated with detrimental side-effects. 1 While therapeutic removal of Aβ with antibodies may hold promise, its beneficial effects have been modest, 2 – 6 and its side effects resulting from its removal from cerebral vasculature potentially devastating. 7 Conceptually innovative approaches to detoxify aggregated Aβ without physically removing it from the brain are urgently needed. 8 Remodelling of Aβ to non-toxic species without physically removing it from the brain has been tried as an alternative therapeutic strategy. Implementation approaches include the use of cucurbit[8]uril, orcein-related O4, 9 scyllo-inositol, 10 tramiprosate, 11 resveratrol, 12 , 13 as well as diverse L- and D-peptides. 14 These molecules interact with Aβ through non-covalent binding and alter its properties. Although some of the clinical trials are still ongoing (e.g. ALZ-801/valiltramiprosate 15 , 16 ), results suggest that these approaches have significant limitations. For example, Aβ remodelling through supramolecular (non-covalent) interactions are often reversible. 17 Covalent modifications (chemical editing) of Aβ could be a powerful tool to modulate toxic properties of Aβ irreversibly that does not require Aβ clearance from the brain. This approach has not been explored enough yet. Our past work has shown that chiral editing of Serine26 (Ser/S26) reduces Aβ’s fibrillization and produces non-toxic-soluble aggregates. This study also found that S26s, replacement of L-Ser with D-Ser interferes with the side chain-to-side chain H-bonding of Asparagine27 (Asn/N27) with N27 of the neighbouring layers in Aβ fibrils. These intermolecular H-bonding interaction through amide groups of the N27 side chain has a critical involvement in Aβ’s aggregation properties. 18 19 – 21 Based on our prior findings implicating the importance of N27, we rationalized in the present study that deamidation of the N27 side chain would produce similar interference to the interfibrillar interactions between N27 side chains and could play a critical role in controlling Aβ’s toxic and aggregation properties. To test our hypothesis, we mutated N27 to Aspartate (Asp/D) in Aβ42 by amino acid substitution during its synthesis (N27D), producing the peptide that would results from deamidation of N27. This N27D variant of Aβ has been found in human AD brain extracts as well as in the brains of Aβ-expressing 5xFAD mice. 22 However, the impact of N27D on Aβ aggregation and toxicity and its physiological relevance to AD remain poorly understood. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of how N27D impacts Aβ42 aggregation, toxicity and other cellular effects using biological and biophysical methods. We found that N27D reduced Aβ’s neurotoxicity, microglial proinflammatory cytokine production, and oligomerization. These data suggest that deamidation may be a therapeutically relevant approach for Alzheimer’s treatment. 22 Results Aβ42-N27D is less neurotoxic than Aβ42-WT We began by investigating micromolar neurotoxicity of Aβ42 and comparing it with the deamidated variant, Aβ42-N27D. We incubated human neuroblastoma (SH-SY5Y) cells with Aβ42-WT or Aβ42-N27D at 20 μM or 50 μM, for 72 h and determined cell viability by the WST-1 assay as in our prior studies. We found Aβ42-N27D to be significantly less toxic than Aβ42-WT. Specifically, Aβ42-WT reduced cell viability to 48.7 ± 0.024 % at 20 μM and 25.7 ± 0.029 % at 50 μM, while Aβ42-N27D reduced cell viability to 80.9 ± 0.020 % at 20 μM and 76.9 ± 0.023 % cell viability at 50 μM ( 18 Figure 1b ). The biological replicates are provided in the Supplemental Information ( Figure S1 ). Different synthetic batches of Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D were compared and gave consistent results ( Figure S2 ). The nature of biological actions of Aβ42 depends on concentration. We therefore next examined the neurotoxic effects of Aβ on primary mouse cortical neurons. Given the known higher sensitivity of the primary neurons to Aβ42 toxicity than SH-SY5Y, these experiments were conducted at low nanomolar concentration of the peptides in question. 23 Primary cortical mouse neurons were treated with Aβ42-WT or Aβ42-N27D (50 nM, 24 h) and viability determined using Live/Dead cytotoxicity test. As others have found 24 , 25 , Aβ42-WT significantly reduced neuronal viability compared to control vehicle ( 26 , 27 Figure 1c ). Aβ42-N27D, on the other hand, was not toxic under those conditions, which is consistent with our micromolar findings in SH-SY5Y cells. The individual biological replicates and the representative microscopic images of the treated cells are provided in the Supplemental Information ( Figure S3 ). Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D have differential effects on microglial cytokine expression Dysregulated microglial immune function is a central feature of AD pathogenesis and can be driven in part by toxic Aβ42 oligomers. To test for potential differences in microglial response between Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D, we exposed mouse microglial cells (MMC) to the peptides (50 nM 28 , 24 h). We then used a Luminex multiplexed immunoassay to quantify a panel of 30 pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokines secreted into the culture medium ( 24 Figure 2a ). We accounted for the multivariate nature of the data using a discriminant partial least squares regression (D-PLSR) ( 29 Figure S4a , S4b ), which separated Aβ42-WT conditioned wells to the right and vehicle and Aβ42-N27D to the left along the horizontal axis, called Latent Variable 1 (LV1). LV1 consisted of a weighted profile of cytokines ( Figure S4b ) correlated with Aβ42-WT (positive) or with vehicle or Aβ42-N27D (negative). The magnitude of each weight indicates how important that cytokine is in the separation along LV1 (similar to a principal component analysis). A pronounced difference was observed in the expression levels of the proinflammatory cytokine IL-6, which was induced (~ 2.7-fold) by Aβ42-WT, but not by Aβ42-N27D ( Figure 2b ). Swarm plots showing univariate analysis for 30 cytokines are provided in the Supplemental Information ( Figure S5 ). Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D have distinct fibrillization profiles One of the hallmark features of Aβ aggregation is fibril formation. We examined the impact of the N27D chemical edit on Aβ42 fibrillization using the Thioflavin-T (ThT) fibril formation assay and our previously reported conditions. Aβ42-WT rapidly aggregated with an aggregation half-time of t 1/2 ~22–40.8 min whereas Aβ42-N27D exhibited a remarkable slowing in aggregation with a t 1/2 ~256–342 min ( Figure 3a ). This trend is consistent with the studies reported before. The ThT-monitored aggregation profile of Aβ42-N27D also exhibited a very long lag phase before entering the elongation/growth phase. Aβ42-WT exhibits a very short lag phase of ~11–11.5 min, and Aβ42-N27D has this phase last ~127–205 min. Addition of preformed N27D fibril seeds to aggregating Aβ42-WT showed no noticeable effect ( 30 , 31 Figure S6 ). Mixing of the two peptides immediately upon reconstitution on the other hand had substantial impact on fibrillization, showing the two peptides are capable of interacting with each other under adequately selected conditions ( Figure S7 ). Fluorescence quenching in aggregating 5(6)-carboxytetramethylrhodamine (TAMRA)-labelled Aβ can provide insights into aggregation intermediates that are not detectable by the ThT assay. We synthesized N-terminally TAMRA-labelled Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D (TAMRA-Aβ42-WT and TAMRA-Aβ42-N27D, respectively). Fluorescence of TAMRA-Aβ42-WT was fully quenched within ~60 min ( 32 Figure 3b ), with an aggregation profile quantitatively similar to that seen in the ThT fibril formation experiments performed with Aβ42-WT ( Figure 3a ). In contrast, TAMRA-Aβ42-N27D followed a more complex aggregation profile ( Figure 3b ). A partial fluorescence quenching occurred within ~45 min, followed by a plateau that persisted for ~3 h indicating the formation of a soluble aggregation intermediate. This intermediate was subsequently converted into final aggregated form as indicated by the increased fluorescence quenching after ~4 h of aggregation. Consistent trends were observed with batches from different sources ( 18 Figure S8 ). Aβ42-N27D forms fewer oligomers than Aβ42-WT To quantify oligomer distribution in freshly reconstituted Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D we next used photo-induced cross-linking of unmodified proteins (PICUP). The oligomer distribution within Aβ42-WT was found to be in good agreement with previous reports. 33 , 34 The oligomer distribution within Aβ42-N27D was very similar to that of Aβ42-WT ( 33 Figure 4a ). A change in peptide concentration (20, 50 μM) only had a subtle effect on relative oligomer distribution that could not be quantified. Non-crosslinked Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D were also analysed by SDS-PAGE electrophoresis ( Figure 4b ), revealing a clear presence of higher molecular weight aggregates with Aβ42-WT, but not with Aβ42-N27D. Also, we noticed that Aβ42-N27D aggregates are more susceptible to protease digestion compared to Aβ42-WT, implying their differential stability. To further quantify the low-molecular weight (MW) intermediate populations in Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D we used Size-Exclusion Chromatography (SEC). Elution profiles of Aβ42-WT (grey) and Aβ42-N27D (blue) are shown in 35 Figure 4c ( Cf. Figure S9 ). The peaks eluting at ~7–11 mL correspond to the 2–8-mer aggregates and the peaks eluting at ~12–15 mL correspond to the monomer peptides. The Aβ42-WT peptide displayed a higher relative quantity of the oligomer fraction than Aβ42-N27D upon reconstitution. When incubated for 24 h at 37 °C, both samples showed declining oligomer quantities, with some shift towards more aggregated soluble intermediates, consistent with advancing fibrillization of the two samples. 36 Discussion Protein sidechain deamidation is a common post-translational modification that can happen enzymatically or non-enzymatically. Deamidation of Asn/N has been studied in many proteins , and has been shown to impact diverse biological functions, including protein turnover, aging etc. 37 – 43 Deamidated Aβ has been found in human AD brain extracts. 44 – 47 Bugrova et al. shows that deamidated peptide is present in brains of Aβ-expressing 5xFAD mice. 22 While these studies suggest physiological relevance for protein deamidation generally, and its potential relevance to AD more specifically, the impact of Aβ deamidation on peptide aggregation and toxicity had remained poorly understood prior to this study. 22 We found that the N27D sidechain deamidation of Aβ42 significantly reduces its neurotoxicity and other potentially detrimental cellular effects and alters its aggregation propensity. A substantial reduction in soluble oligomers of Aβ42 was observed upon N27D modification. Interestingly, the N27D sidechain edit led to a selective reduction of microglial cytokine release, which was particularly notable with the important pro-inflammatory cytokine, IL6. These findings present a novel approach to attenuate potentially detrimental effects of Aβ that does not require removing it from brain and can thereby avoid potentially harmful side effects associated with that removal. Our cell culture experiments found that the N27D sidechains modification reduced Aβ42 cytotoxicity ( Figure 1 ). The impact of the two peptides on cell viability was tested in SH-SY5Y cells and primary neurons. Cell viability experiments in the SH-SY5Y model cell line were conducted first ( Figure 1b ) and showed a robust reduction in neurotoxicity upon N27D modification at micromolar concentrations. We therefore further evaluated the potential protective effects of N27D using primary mouse cortical neurons ( Figure 1c ), which corroborated our SH-SY5Y findings, at much lower (nanomolar) concentrations, using the live/dead cell staining. WST-1 measures metabolic activity by mitochondrial enzymes, whereas the live/dead cell stain measures membrane integrity. Both assays gave consistent results, indicating that the N27D sidechain edit may make Aβ42 less neurotoxic. We investigated the impact of N27D on immunomodulatory responses. We examined Aβ induced inflammatory response in microglia, quantified the release of inflammatory cytokines secreted, and analysed the differential response upon treatment with Aβ42-N27D ( Figure 2a , 2b ). Our results showed that a cluster of proinflammatory cytokines was downregulated in the Aβ42-N27D variant compared to Aβ42-WT. IL-6, a proinflammatory cytokine, was significantly downregulated in Aβ42-N27D relative to Aβ42-WT ( Figure 2b ). Elevated IL-6 levels have been associated with Alzheimer’s disease where they positively correlated with memory impairment, and can stimulate glial cells to release a cascade of proinflammatory cytokines. 48 Most notably we found that the important pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6, which was robustly induced by Aβ42-WT was not induced by Aβ42-N27D ( 49 Figure 2b ). Several other cytokines showed a trend toward decreased induction by Aβ42-N27D compared to Aβ42-WT. Among these was G-CSF, a cytokine known for its role in activating immune cells ( Figure 2b ). The overexpression of these two cytokines could result in immune cell signalling dysregulation. Hence, the reduced ability of Aβ42-N27D to induce the expression of these proinflammatory cytokines could potentially mitigate chronic inflammation in patients with Alzheimer’s or other Aβ-associated diseases. A potential limitation of this study was the use of an immortalized microglial cell line, as these cells differ from primary cells due to genetic alterations and possibly altered responses, making them potentially less representative of normal cellular behaviour. Future studies will utilize primary microglial cells, and co-cultures with other primary neural cell types to further understand the Aβ42-N27D’s role in an environment more reflective of the brain. Despite this limitation, the Aβ42-N27D variant seemed to elicit a distinct microglial response from Aβ42-WT. While microglial responses are essential for Aβ clearance and plaque containment, prolonged interaction with Aβ can lead to sustained production of proinflammatory factors, such as IL-6, and result in microglial priming. 50 Chronic inflammation produced by long-term microglial activation can promote neuronal damage disease pathogenesis. 51 Our finding that upregulation of some pro-inflammatory cytokines by Aβ is mitigated by N27D suggests that inducing this amino acid modification may lessen Aβ’s detrimental cellular effects. 52 Given previous reports on the relationship between cytotoxicity and aggregation, we examined the effect of N27D on Aβ aggregation. We witnessed a significant decrease in aggregation propensity and reduced oligomer formation in Aβ42-N27D relative to Aβ42-WT ( 18 , 53 , 54 Figure 3 , 4 ). In Aβ42 fibrils, N27 engages in intermolecular amide sidechain to sidechain H-bonding to N27 in the neighbouring Aβ peptides. N27 deamidation renders the sidechain incapable of such interactions and could alter aggregation. Similarly, our prior studies reported that replacement of L-Ser with D-Ser at position 26 of Aβ42 also inhibits this intermolecular bond by promoting intra-chain H-bonding between D-Ser and the adjacent N27 instead. 19 , 21 This D-Ser26 substitution likewise results in suppression of Aβ42’s toxicity and delayed amyloid aggregation. 18 In addition, Aβ42-N27D increases the negative charges on Aβ42, enabling a potential intra-peptide electrostatic interaction between D27 and Lysine28 (K28). This could interfere with K28-A42 bridge present in the unmodified Aβ42 fibril. 18 Overall, these alterations could change the fibrillization and biological properties of Aβ42. In contrast to D-Ser substitution, however, N27 deamidation offers a more tangible therapeutic potential as there are FDA-approved asparaginase enzymes that are used in cancer chemotherapy.. Studies using in vivo models of Aβ42 toxicity as a function of N27D modification, as well as the neuronal expression of potentially protective asparaginase constructs, may help contextualize the translational relevance of this approach in the future. 20 , 21 The substantially reduced peak intensity corresponding to oligomers (2–8-mer) in the SEC profile for Aβ42-N27D ( Figure 4c , Cf. Figure S10 ) suggests that the N27D sidechain edit interferes with Aβ42’s oligomerization, and that the two peptides may have a differential stability with regard to enzymatic processing. Such interference could possibly occur due to the altered conformational and electrostatic interactions discussed above. This reduction in oligomers is present in both early (lag) and terminal fibril formation phases of the Aβ aggregation kinetics (0 and 24 h incubation). This observation suggests that the impact of N27D on oligomer formation is maintained throughout the Aβ aggregation process, rather than merely reflecting particular portions of it that may be enriched or depleted in oligomer formation. The unnormalized SEC profile ( Figure S9 ) for both Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D (24 h incubation) exhibited reduced intensities for both oligomer and monomer peaks, consistent with the formation of larger fibrillar species excluded from the SEC column. Nevertheless, the relative difference in oligomer content between Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D was maintained. Both Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D were found to bind the antibodies 6E10, 4G8 and OC to a similar extent ( Figure S11 ). Taken together, our analyses of Aβ42-N27D biophysical properties suggests that it alters the aggregation process at all stages, including early events presumptively leading to oligomer formation, as well as later stages of fibril formation. Aβ42-N27D also exhibited a prolonged lag phase in ThT fibril formation assay and TAMRA quenching assays compared to Aβ42-WT ( Figure 3a , 3b ). Structural events occurring during the lag phase are largely unknown. However, the longer lag phase has been previously interpreted to indicate metastable intermediates and their conformational reorganizations. These pre-fibrillar intermediates could be either on-pathway fibril precursors or off-pathway fibril competitors. 55 A longer lag phase seen in Aβ42-N27D could imply two different possibilities: either hindered formation of on-pathway oligomers that act as precursors for seeding fibrils or increased formation of off-pathway oligomers that do not aid in fibril formation and growth, but scavenge the monomers available for fibril formation. The observation in SEC supports Aβ42-N27D’s impaired ability to form on-pathway transient species, which further seeds fibrils. The peak corresponding to oligomers (2–8-mer) in SEC profile for Aβ42-WT at 0 h consists of two components, an earlier small shoulder peak followed by a later more intense peak. The earlier shoulder peak consists of higher molecular weight oligomers relative to the later peak, with the earlier peak intensifying and the later one disappearing after 24 h. This suggests that Aβ42-N27D preferentially inhibits low-MW versus high-MW oligomer formation. Therefore, the preferential inhibition of low-MW oligomers and delayed fibrillization of Aβ42-N27D could be speculated as Aβ42-N27D’s differential pathway of aggregation compared to Aβ42-WT. Future studies are needed to determine the mechanistic details of these effects and structural characterization to clarify how this variation is manifested as distinct cellular toxicity and responses. Nevertheless, the decrease in toxic oligomer content seen in Aβ42-N27D could help explain the observed differences in neurotoxicity and immunomodulation. For example, oligomers have been shown to induce more neuronal toxicity compared to other Aβ aggregates. 56 Aβ aggregates in general, and oligomers in particular, also stimulate the production of IL-6 by microglia. 57 Thus, oligomer reduction in Aβ42-N27D may contribute to the differential microglial response and the downregulation of proinflammatory cytokines seen in our analyses. Altogether, our results expand available information of how not just deamidation, but also other post-translational modification of Aβ can impact its potentially detrimental cellular effects and yield broader insight into these and potentially other effects. 58 Specific toxicity-altering modifications are already known in the context of Aβ mutations causing dominantly inherited forms of amyloidosis (AD and cerebral amyloid angiopathy; CAA). For example, there are three dominantly inherited AD (DIAD) mutations in Aβ that lead to an increase in the number of residues with side chain amidation (Asp/D or Glutamate (Glu/E) residues to Asn/N or Glutamine (Gln/Q) respectively): D7N (Tottori), E22Q (Dutch), and D23N (Iowa). These mutations generate forms of Aβ that are more neurotoxic and fibrilize faster than wildtype Aβ. Given the potential role of amidation in familial Aβ mutants’ s structure and/or toxicity, it is relevant to see how sidechain edit by N27 deamidation reduces toxicity and potentially harmful effects of Aβ. 59 – 65 Numerous strategies to treat AD by targeting Aβ have been explored clinically, most successfully by removing it from the brain using anti-Aβ antibodies. Although two FDA-approved Aβ antibodies have shown moderate benefits for patients to date, removal of cerebral Aβ can result in vascular leakage and serious adverse events associated with brain haemorrhage. 2 Thus, decreasing the toxicity of Aβ in the brain without damaging brain tissue is a critical unresolved need in AD treatment. Modulating Aβ’s properties through sidechain editing such as N27 deamidation may provide a future basis for therapeutic benefits to Alzheimer’s patients by decreasing Aβ oligomer formation that underlie its detrimental effects on neurons, microglia, and possibly other cells in AD. In contrast to other modifications, deamidation is both irreversible and genetically encodable. 8 While this unique characteristic made this study and future translational studies readily feasible, it is not directly applicable to the development of human therapies. The availability of biochemical deamidation machinery, however, namely the asparaginase enzyme, may enable assessment of clinical and therapeutic applications in the future. In this context, asparaginase has been previously approved by FDA and used for clinical treatment in human patients. 66 Conclusions Altogether, we showed that the substantial reduction of Aβ’s detrimental cellular effects, its aggregation behaviour, and propensity to form soluble oligomers is possible by effectively deamidating the N27 sidechain. This also highlights the critical contribution of N27 residue to Aβ’s toxic and aggregation properties. As such, N27 deamidation, and sidechain deamidation generally, represents an intriguing target for possible therapeutic intervention. Further research is needed to explore the full potential of this approach, either alone or in combination with existing Aβ removal strategies, to develop safer and more effective treatment strategies for Alzheimer’s disease. Experimental Section Additional experimental methods can be found in the Supporting Information . Materials. All commercially available reagents and solvents were used as received without further purification. Peptide Synthesis. All peptides were synthesized by SPPS using Fmoc chemistry, following our previously published protocols51 on Tentagel ® S PHB resin (Rapp Polymere GmbH, cat no. RA1327). Peptides purchased from AnaSpec, Inc. (Fremont, CA) have also been used. Peptide Purification and Preparation. The peptides were cleaved from the resin and deprotected using a cleavage cocktail solution of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA, 10 mL), triisopropylsilane (1 mL) and liquified phenol (0.5 mL). The lyophilized peptides were dissolved in H 2 O with 0.1% NH 4 OH and purified using reverse-phase HPLC and PLRP-S columns (8 μm, 300 Å) under basic conditions as previously reported. All peptide purities range from 97–99%. The concentration of Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D were determined at 280 nm (ε = 1490 M-1 cm-1) using Nanodrop. Cell Culture. Human neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y cells were purchased from ATCC and cultured in 1:1 DMEM: F12 K media supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Cell Viability (WST-1 Assay). WST-1 is a colorimetric assay that measures cleavage of WST-1 into a formazan by mitochondrial dehydrogenases in live cells. SH-SY5Y cells were plated in a 96-well plate at a density of 50,000 cells/well (100 μL total volume/well) and allowed to adhere for 24 h before dosing. Lyophilized peptides were dissolved in 15 μL of 20 mM NaOH and the solutions were diluted to a final concentration of 50 μM with culture media and added to cells. Cells were incubated for 72 h at 37 °C after dosing with the samples. At the end of the incubation period, 10 μL aliquots of WST-1 (Roche) were added to each well and incubated for 3 h. Absorbance was measured at λ = 490 nm for the WST-1 reading. Each bar represents an average of five technical replicates, normalized against the vehicle (cells and media only). Neuron Cell Cultures. Neuron cultures were derived from E14–15 CD1 mice (Charles River Laboratories) under a protocol approved by the Georgia Institute of Technology Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee. Cortices were isolated according to an existing protocol and triturated in warm plating medium using a 1 mL pipette tip. Plating medium consisted of Neurobasal Plus Medium (Gibco), with 10 % FBS, 1x Glutamax (Gibco), and 1x antibiotic solution (Sigma). Cell concentration was measured using a hemocytometer and cells were plated at 0.04*10^6 cells/mL in 0.1% PEI (Sigma)-coated 96 well plates. Cells were left to attach overnight in plating medium, then switched to neuron medium containing 1x B27 supplement (Gibco) instead of FBS. Half medium changes then took place on day 4 and day 7 in culture. 67 Live/Dead Cytotoxicity Assay. Cells were treated with either 50 nM Aβ42-WT, 50 nM Aβ42-N27D, or vehicle (200 nM NaOH) for 24h. Viability was established through the ThermoFisher LIVE/DEAD @ Viability/Cytotoxicity Kit. Cells were incubated with 4mM Calcein AM in DMSO and 2mM Ethidium homodimer in DMSO for 30 min to assess the Live/Dead ratio. After incubation, the plate was read on a microplate reader at 530 nm and 645 nm, and the fluorescent intensity (F) was recorded. Mouse Microglial Cells (MMCs) Culture. MMCs were cultured in DMEM-F12 medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % Penicillin-Streptomycin until they reached 70% confluency. Cells were stored in an incubator with 95 % air and 5 % CO2 atmosphere at 37 °C. Upon reaching 70% confluency, cells were seeded into 6-well plates and incubated for 24 h. Cells were treated with either 50 nM Aβ42-WT, 50 nM Aβ42-N27D, or vehicle (200 nM NaOH) for 24 h followed by medium collection. Collected medium was centrifuged at 13,000 RPM for 10 min at 4°C, then supernatant was collected and stored at −80 °C. The experiment was conducted in two separate runs and data were combined. Multivariate Analysis of Cytokine Data. Dunn’s tests were performed in R and adjusted using a Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Discriminant partial least squares regression (D-PLSR) was performed in R using the ropls package v3.19. The data were z-scored before input into the function. Cytokine measurements were used as the independent variables, and the treatment group was used as the discrete regression variable in all D-PLSR analyses. Orthogonal rotations were applied to the sample scores and analyte weightings to obtain separation of each group along the LV1 and LV2 axes. Error bars for LV loadings were computed by iteratively excluding K samples without replacement 100 times (leave-K-out cross-validation, LKOCV), and regenerating the D-PLSR model each time. Error bars in the LV and LV2 plots report the mean and SD computed across the models generated to provide an indication of the variability within each cytokine. 68 , 69 ThT Assay and TAMRA Quenching Assay. The ThT assay was conducted as described previously. The methodological details are given in the 18 , 33 , 70 Supporting Information . Fibril Growth. Lyophilized peptide Aβ42-WT or Aβ42-N27D was dissolved in 20 mM NaOH (less than 3% of the total volume is used) and sonicated for 30 s and then diluted to desired concentration in PBS. The samples were incubated at 37 °C quiescently for 48 hours. Protease Digestion of Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D Aggregates using Proteinase K. Lyophilized peptide was dissolved as described above and diluted to a concentration of 200 μg/mL in PBS. The samples were incubated at 37 °C quiescently for 48 hours. Digestion was carried out by the addition of 0.4 μL of proteinase K (20 mg/mL) and aliquots were collected at different time points and protease inhibitor was added to the aliquots following previously reported protocol. Each aliquot (25 μL) was mixed with loading buffer containing 5% 2-mercaptoethanol and analysed by running SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis (using 4–20% gradient gel) according to the protocol reported previously. 71 18 Dot Blot Assay. Aβ42-WT or Aβ42-N27D (20 μM) samples were prepared and kept at 37 °C. 2 μL of sample was spotted on nitrocellulose membrane at different time points and dried them fully. The spotted membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk in TBST for 1 h at 25 °C. Then, samples were washed thrice with TBST for 5 mins. Later, they were incubated with primary antibody (1:1000 in 5% non-fat dry milk in TBST) at 4 °C for overnight. After washing with TBST, the membranes were incubated with the secondary antibody (1:5000 in 5% non-fat dry milk in TBST) for 1 h at 25 °C, washed with TBST for 5 min × 3, followed by development with the Opti-4CN Substrate kit (Bio-Rad, cat no. 1708235). Photochemically Induced Crosslinking of Unmodified Peptides (PICUP). 4 μL of 1 mM [Ru(bipy) 3 ] 2+ and 4 μL of 20 mM ammonium persulfate were added to 32 μL aliquots of 20 μM Aβ42-WT and Aβ42-N27D in PBS. The samples were irradiated for 1.2 s with white light using our previously described setup. Following irradiation, the samples were immediately quenched with 40 μL of loading buffer containing 5% 2-mercaptoethanol and separated by SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis (12 % tris-tricine polyacrylamide) at 100 V for 2 h. The gels were developed by silver staining. 18 , 34 Size Exclusion Chromatography (SEC). The HPLC-purified monomers patterns of Aβ 42-WT and Aβ-N27D peptides (2 mg) were dissolved in 1 mL hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP) and subjected to lyophilization overnight. The lyophilized peptides were reconstituted by dissolving in 100 μL 0.5 M sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and 400 μL 20 mM sodium phosphate buffer (NaPi). It was followed by centrifugation at 15,000 ×g for 15 min to remove any insoluble aggregates. Prior to injection, the samples were filtered through a 0.22 μm PES filter and then injected the resulting solution immediately into a 500 μL loop connected to SEC system equipped with a Superdex 75 10/300 GL column (Cytiva). For time-dependent studies, the lyophilized peptides were dissolved in 20 mM NaPi and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h prior to SEC analysis. The elution of both peptides was monitored at 280 nm. The chromatographic separation was performed at room temperature on the Superdex 75 column. We identified oligomeric species based on their elution volumes, with 7–11 mL corresponding to 2–8mer aggregates and 12–15 mL representing monomeric species, as previously established in the literature. 72 Supplementary Material Supporting Information: General experimental procedures; batch-to-batch results, additional figures ( figures S1 – S11 ) as mentioned in the text.
